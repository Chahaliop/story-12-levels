# Story 12 Levels

A script is ready only when a stranger understands it in 3 seconds AND it clears all 12 story levels. Ticking the 12 levels alone is not enough: plenty of "perfect" scripts fail because they're too complex. So simplicity comes first, structure second, and proof (the scorecard) last.

## The order of work (always in this order)

1. **Idea test** (before writing anything). Write the idea as one line of fact. Score it, ideally with a few independent reviewer agents (a random stranger, a target viewer, a top creator, a retention analyst), from 1 to 10 on "would you stop, would you tell a friend?". Only script ideas that average **7+**. See `references/idea-test.md`.
2. **One sentence.** Write the whole video in one sentence. Give it to a fresh reader with no context and ask "what is this about?". If they can't say it back, simplify before going on.
3. **The 10 questions + Problem / Pursuit / Payoff.** Answer them before drafting (`references/12-levels.md`, section "10 questions").
4. **Shots first.** For video, fill in the SEE column (one shot every 1 to 2 seconds) before writing any voice-over. Read only the SEE column: if the story makes sense muted, add words. See `references/shots-and-hooks.md`.
5. **15 hooks → score → blind test the top 5 → pick.** Never trust the first hook.
6. **Draft in the simple voice** (`references/simple-voice.md`), with emotion tags, `(pause)` marks and one CAPS stress word.
7. **X-ray all 12 levels**, fix what fails, re-check earlier levels after each fix (fixes interact).
8. **Fact-check** every detail, especially the vivid show-don't-tell ones.
9. **Count words and time it.** About 140 to 150 words per 60 seconds.
10. **Hand over**: the script as readable text in chat (not only a file), the 12-level scorecard underneath, the facts still to verify, and a cut list for if it runs long.

## The simplicity rules (these beat everything else)

- **ONE idea, ONE question.** At most **3 numbers**, and no maths for the viewer to do.
- **Line 1 says the topic and the promise plainly.** "X costs $400. Here's where that money goes." No riddle or joke the viewer has to decode. The viewer must know what it's about and what they'll get within 2 to 3 seconds.
- **Present tense**, told as it happens to the character ("Rome burns for six days", not "Rome burned").
- **Joined by "but" and "so"**, never "and then".
- **Talk like a friend.** If a 12-year-old wouldn't get a word, swap it. Lines of 4 to 10 words, one idea per line.
- **Start from something everyone already knows** to explain the strange thing.
- **Ask the question out loud, then answer it.** The answer is the payoff.
- **Every viewer leaves with 4 things**: 1 number, 1 simple why, 1 surprise, 1 line they'd repeat to a friend.

## The 12 levels (short form; full checkpoints in `references/12-levels.md`)

| # | Level | The rule in one line |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Problem → Pursuit → Payoff | The viewer's problem in the first 1 to 3 s, a pursuit that adds something new every beat, a payoff that answers the exact question the hook opened. |
| L2 | Want + Stakes + Conflict | Someone wants something, something is at risk, something is in the way. Say the viewer's own stakes. |
| L3 | But / Therefore | Every join is but, so, because, except. Zero "and then". |
| L4 | Curiosity loops | Always one question open. Open the next loop before closing the current one. Delay the reveal word to the end of the sentence. |
| L5 | Show vs tell | Replace every adjective with evidence you can picture: a number, a name, an object, an action. One strong sourced detail per beat. |
| L6 | Escalation + reversals | Each beat goes one step higher. One big honest objection (the drop), then evidence weak → strong → strongest, biggest reversal at the end. |
| L7 | Transformation + theme | Before belief → after belief. Theme is one universal sentence, planted early, proven in the middle, said once. Add an irony beat if the story has one. |
| L8 | Scenes + visuals | 2 to 3 real scenes (place, time, person, action, turn). Every line has a picture that adds, not repeats. One motif that returns and changes meaning. |
| L9 | Pacing | Fewest words. Cut any line the picture or on-screen text already carries. About 4 pauses per 60 s, only at reveals. Something new every 1.5 to 3 s. |
| L10 | Hook + ending | Hook works muted, as text and as voice in 3 s, and can't be guessed. Ending pays off the hook exactly, with a callback to its words, and ends on the strongest line. |
| L11 | Edu + business value | The lesson comes out of the story. A named tool they can save. Raise the strongest objection yourself. Visible sources. A reason to share and to comment. |
| L12 | X-ray + own shape | Borrow the shape of great stories, never their content. X-ray your own draft and fix the weakest 2 levels. |

## The scorecard (paste under EVERY script)

```
12-LEVEL SCORECARD — <title>
ONE SENTENCE: …   (blind reader said back: "…")
IDEA SCORE: …/10
L1  3 P's ............................ ✅  Problem: … / Pursuit: … / Payoff: …
L2  Want + Stakes + Conflict ......... ✅  … wants … / loses … / blocked by …
L3  But / Therefore .................. ✅  "and then" joins left: 0
L4  Curiosity loops .................. ✅  loops: … ; longest gap: … s ; delayed reveal: "…"
L5  Show vs tell ..................... ✅  tells left: 0 ; key details: … (sourced)
L6  Escalation + reversals ........... ✅  drop: "…" ; climb: … → … → … ; final reframe: "…"
L7  Transformation + theme ........... ✅  before: … / after: … / theme: … / irony: …
L8  Scenes + visuals ................. ✅  scenes: … ; motif: … ; every line has a picture
L9  Pacing ........................... ✅  words: … / … s ; pauses: … ; cut list: …
L10 Hook + ending .................... ✅  15 hooks, picked #… ; callback: "…"
L11 Edu + business ................... ✅  tool: … ; sources: … ; share line: …
L12 X-ray + own shape ................ ✅  10 questions answered ; weakest 2 fixed: …
FACTS TO VERIFY: …
```

- **Never hand over a ❌.** Fix it first.
- **Mark ⚠️ instead of ✅** when you're unsure a level passes. Honesty beats a clean-looking card.
- If a level truly can't apply, write "n/a, because …". Never leave one blank.

## Format notes

- **Shorts (30 to 60 s):** follow the beat sheet in `references/12-levels.md`. For 30 to 40 s Shorts keep the voice-over at about 90 to 110 words.
- **Long form (8 to 20 min):** the same 12 levels at two scales. The whole video has one main loop and one big drop around 60 to 70% in. Every 2 to 3 minute chapter is its own mini-story. Re-hook every 60 to 90 s.
- **Calm or sleep content:** escalate wonder, not tension. Soft reversals ("and yet…"), about 120 to 130 wpm, an ending that settles.
- **Clips from podcasts or interviews:** you can't change their words, so apply the levels through **selection** (pick a moment that already has a problem, a turn and a payoff) and the **edit** (hook text, B-roll scenes, a freeze or rewind on the reveal, a lesson card).

## Honesty rules that never change

- Every fact is sourced. Rumours are labelled as rumours. Estimates say "about". Never claim more than the source says.
- The hook must be exactly paid off. No overpromising.
- Stay neutral where a topic divides people: explain the move, not the person.
