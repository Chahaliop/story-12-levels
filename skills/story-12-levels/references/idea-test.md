# The idea test: before any script

Most videos fail at the idea, not the script. So test the idea as one line of fact before writing a single word of script.

## How to run it

1. Find about 5 candidate ideas. Write each as **one plain line of fact** with a named, sourced example ("A store kept selling a scratch ticket after its top prize was already won").
2. Put the facts in a notes-free file: no script, no hints, no hype.
3. Give it to **4 independent reviewers** (separate agents with no shared context), each playing a different viewer:
   - a random stranger scrolling (about 24 years old)
   - a typical target viewer (for example, a 41-year-old parent)
   - a top creator in the niche
   - a retention analyst
4. Each scores every idea from 1 to 10 on "would you stop scrolling, and would you tell a friend?", with one line on why.
5. Average the scores and report them honestly. **Only script an idea that averages 7 or more.** If none reach 7, find new ideas and run another round. Don't script a 6 and hope.

## What makes a 7+ idea (learned the hard way)

- **The viewer is the victim.** "YOU paid, and somebody quietly kept the difference" beats any fact about someone else's cost. Facts about other people's money top out at 5 to 6.
- **In a place they know, today, in one picture.** In a shop, on their phone bill, at the checkout.
- **Something they did NOT already suspect.** "Deals are fake" only confirms a suspicion and scores low. A hidden HOW with a current, named example scores high.
- **Named and sourced beats vague.** Words like "some" or "sometimes" cost a point. Reviewers want one real example they could check.
- **Plain couch words.** No spec jargon in the idea line.

## After the test

Take the winning idea into the one-sentence test, then the 10 questions and the 12 levels.
