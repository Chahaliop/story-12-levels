# The 12 levels in full

For each level: the rule, the checkpoints to tick, the fail signs, the fix and an example. The running example is a 60-second history Short, "Did Jesus really live?", told through the Roman historian Tacitus (full script in `example.md`).

## The 12 levels: rule, checkpoints, fail signs, fix, example

### LEVEL 1: Problem → Pursuit → Payoff (the 3 P's)
**Rule:** every script has a problem the viewer recognises, a pursuit that moves, and a payoff that answers the problem.
**Checkpoints:**
- [ ] The problem is the **viewer's** problem or question, not "our topic".
- [ ] It's **specific** (a picture, not "kids can be hard").
- [ ] It lands in the **first 1–3 s** (Shorts) or first 30 s (Long).
- [ ] The pursuit adds something **new** every beat and always ties back to the problem.
- [ ] The payoff has all 3 layers: **consequence** (what happened), **change** (what's different now), **meaning** (what to do or believe).
- [ ] The payoff answers **the exact question** the hook opened.

**Fail signs:** starts with background or the solution; "and then… and then"; ends right after the explanation; the payoff answers a different question.
**Fix:** write three lines before drafting, Problem / Pursuit / Payoff. If you can't, the script isn't ready.
**Example:** Problem "Was Jesus made up?" → Pursuit: Tacitus, the fire, the sentence, the doubt, more witnesses → Payoff "the question was never IF he lived; it's WHO he was."

### LEVEL 2: Want + Stakes + Conflict
**Rule:** someone wants something, something is at risk, and something is in the way. Without this, facts are trivia.
**Checkpoints:**
- [ ] Every character in the script has a clear **want** (even a historical figure).
- [ ] The **stakes** are said out loud: what is lost if the want fails.
- [ ] The **viewer's** own stakes are clear (why this matters to *them*).
- [ ] There's **conflict**: external (person vs person or the world), social (side vs side), and ideally **internal** (a person against themselves, the strongest kind).
- [ ] The stakes peak at the **lowest point** of the story (the doubt or drop).

**Fail signs:** a character who only "does" or "writes" things; "so what?" after the hook; no opposing side.
**Fix:** for each character, write "wants ___ / loses ___ if not / blocked by ___".
**Example:**
- Nero wants to survive the rumour: "His throne is in danger."
- Tacitus promised to write "without anger or bias" (Annals 1.1), but he hates Christians. That's the internal conflict.
- The viewer's stakes: "If he's just repeating a rumour… then the world's biggest faith starts with one."

### LEVEL 3: Because / But / Therefore (cause and effect)
**Rule:** between every two beats, the link is **but**, **so/therefore** or **because**, never "and then".
**Checkpoints:**
- [ ] Do the **"and then" test** out loud on every join. Any join where "and then" sounds natural fails.
- [ ] Every beat is **caused** by the beat before it.
- [ ] You can retell the whole story in one breath using only *but* and *so*.
- [ ] Nothing can be cut without breaking the chain.

**Fail signs:** facts stacked in a list; a new section starting with no reason ("When he was a boy, a fire…").
**Fix:** add the missing cause ("He only wrote about Christians… *because of* a fire"), or turn a stack into a turn ("*But* even if you doubt him…").
**Example:** Myth? **But** an enemy wrote about him. **Because** of a fire. People blamed Nero, **so** Nero blamed the Christians. Tacitus hated them, **but** he wrote "Christ". **But** that was 80 years later. **Except** Pilate's name is carved in stone…

### LEVEL 4: Curiosity and information control (open loops)
**Rule:** always keep at least one question open in the viewer's head; open the next one before you close the current one.
**Checkpoints:**
- [ ] There's a **main loop** that runs from the hook to the payoff.
- [ ] **Never more than ~8 s** (Shorts) or ~60 s (Long) without an open loop.
- [ ] The next loop **opens before** the current one closes, so there's no safe exit point.
- [ ] The **key reveal word is delayed**: pause, then the word, at the **end** of the sentence.
- [ ] **Answer partly, then reopen** at least once (a doubt that revives the main question).
- [ ] **Every loop closes**, except one you deliberately hand to the viewer (for comments) or to the next episode.
- [ ] No loop **overpromises**: the payoff gives at least what the loop promised.

**Fail signs:** the answer is guessable from the hook; a reveal said in the first half of a sentence; a dead zone after a loop closes.
**Fix:** draw the loop timeline (open → close for each loop) and fill the gaps.
**Example:** "Their name comes from a man called… (pause) Christ." / "But there's one thing they still fight about… WHO he was."

### LEVEL 5: Specific details, show vs tell
**Rule:** give evidence the viewer can picture or judge themselves, not adjectives.
**Checkpoints:**
- [ ] Every "tell" word (huge, careful, terrible, amazing, very) is **replaced with evidence** (a number, a name, a place, an object, an action, their own words).
- [ ] **One strong detail per beat**, not three weak ones.
- [ ] Details **replace** words, they don't add length.
- [ ] Every detail **serves the story** (it pushes a *but* or *so*), not trivia.
- [ ] Every detail is **fact-checked and sourced**, and rumours are labelled ("people whispered").
- [ ] Each detail is something **you can show on screen**.

**Fail signs:** "He was a careful historian" (tell) or "a huge fire" (tell).
**Fix:** ask "how would the viewer *know* that?" and show that thing instead.
**Example:**
- "Rome burned for six days."
- "He called their faith a 'deadly superstition.'"
- "Pilate's name turned up… carved in stone."

### LEVEL 6: Escalation and reversals
**Rule:** each beat goes one step higher; the viewer's expectation gets flipped at least twice.
**Checkpoints:**
- [ ] **Chart each beat** with a charge (+ / −, toward or away from the answer) and an intensity from 1 to 5.
- [ ] There are **no two beats in a row at the same intensity**.
- [ ] The **expectation is set up** before each reversal (the viewer must believe the first direction).
- [ ] There's **one big drop**: the strongest honest objection, stated strongly.
- [ ] The evidence after the drop goes **weak → strong → strongest**, with the best card last.
- [ ] The **biggest reversal is at the end** (reframing the whole question).

**Fail signs:** three answers at the same level ("3, 3, 3"); a soft doubt ("maybe he repeated it?"); the best evidence used first.
**Fix:** strengthen the objection, reorder the evidence, add the step where the stakes rise.
**Example:** the drop "Tacitus wrote 80 years after Jesus died" → the climb: Pilate Stone → Josephus *20 years earlier* → non-Christian historians agree → the reframe "never IF, it's WHO".
**Sleep/calm channels:** escalate *curiosity and wonder*, not tension. Use soft reversals ("and yet…"), no shocks.

### LEVEL 7: Internal transformation and theme
**Rule:** the viewer (or a character) starts with one belief and leaves with another; the theme is the one truth that change proves.
**Checkpoints:**
- [ ] The **before belief** is stated early, in the viewer's words.
- [ ] The **after belief** is landed clearly at the payoff.
- [ ] The **theme is one sentence**, universal, and true beyond this story.
- [ ] The theme is **planted early, proven in the middle, and said once at the end**, never preached.
- [ ] **Every beat serves the theme** (test each one).
- [ ] There's an **irony** beat (a character's action proves the opposite of what they intended), if the story has one.
- [ ] The theme doesn't take sides where the channel must stay neutral.

**Fail signs:** the ending only restates facts; the theme is a fact ("Jesus was real") or a sermon.
**Fix:** write "Before: ___ / After: ___ / Theme: ___" and cut any beat that doesn't serve it.
**Example:** before "made up by his followers" → after "even his enemies' books record him". Theme: *the strongest proof comes from people who least want to give it.* Irony: "Tacitus never hid his bias. And that's exactly why we trust him."

### LEVEL 8: Scenes and visual storytelling
**Rule:** dramatise the 2–3 key moments as scenes and summarise the rest; every line has a picture that adds, not repeats.
**Checkpoints:**
- [ ] **2–3 scenes** (Shorts) or 1 scene per chapter (Long), each with **place + time + person + action + turn**.
- [ ] The **main reveal happens inside a scene**, not a summary.
- [ ] **Every VO line has a visual cue** written in the script.
- [ ] Each visual **adds** information; it never just illustrates the same word.
- [ ] There's **one visual motif** that returns about 3 times and changes meaning each time.
- [ ] **Colour, sound and music follow the charge**: warm for danger, cold and thin for doubt, bright for the climb.
- [ ] The **reveal word and the reveal image land together**.
- [ ] Visual sources are noted (public domain / licensed / your own).

**Fail signs:** a script with no VISUAL lines; stock "illustration" of every noun; the reveal told over generic b-roll.
**Example:** Scene 1 Rome burning (64 AD); Scene 2 Tacitus at his desk, the pen writes *Christus*; Scene 3 the same page under glass in Florence. Motif: the circled word (hook → reveal → irony).

### LEVEL 9: Pacing and compression
**Rule:** the same story in the fewest words; fast through summary, slow at reveals.
**Checkpoints:**
- [ ] **Word budget:** about 140–150 words per 60 s Short; about 140 wpm for Long. Count them.
- [ ] Every line the **picture or on-screen text already carries** is cut from the voice.
- [ ] **One line per beat**; merge lines that do the same job.
- [ ] All **turn words survive** (but / so / except / instead).
- [ ] **Pause budget:** about 4 pauses per 60 s, only at reveals.
- [ ] **Something new every 1.5–3 s** (Shorts): a picture, a fact or a turn.
- [ ] The timing map is written: which stretches are FAST and which are SLOW.
- [ ] A **cut list** is ready ("if it runs long, cut these lines first").

**Fail signs:** over 60 s with "we'll speed up the voice"; 12 pauses; voice describing what's on screen.
**Fix:** move jobs to the cheapest carrier: picture > on-screen text > implied > voice.
**Fixed audio (podcast or owner voice):** the voice can't be cut, so pacing happens in the pictures (cut rate, holds, freezes).

### LEVEL 10: Hooks and endings
**Rule:** a 3-layer hook that stops the scroll and opens the strongest loop; an ending that pays off, echoes the hook, lands the theme and gives an action.
**Hook checkpoints:**
- [ ] **Visual hook:** frame 1 has a face or motion and works **muted**.
- [ ] **Text hook:** on-screen words carry the hook with no sound.
- [ ] **Verbal hook:** the first line is clear AND curious in 3 s, and the answer **can't be guessed**.
- [ ] The **most interesting thing comes first**, with no intro or background.
- [ ] The hook is **honest** and exactly paid off (no plural you can't deliver, no "proves" you can't back).
- [ ] **No switch dip:** the hook's last line bridges straight into the story with a new loop ("It started… with a fire.").
- [ ] Write **5 hook options** (question / contradiction / in medias res / curiosity object / myth + contradiction), score them, and pick the best.

**Ending checkpoints:**
- [ ] It **pays off the hook** exactly.
- [ ] A **callback** echoes the hook's own words ("IF Jesus was made up…" ↔ "never IF he lived").
- [ ] It **ends on the strongest line**, with no dead air (cut about 0.3 s after the last word).
- [ ] The **CTA has its own mini-hook** (strangest image first) and the next video delivers exactly what it promises.
- [ ] There's a **loop option** for IG/TikTok (last frame matches frame 1), with platform versions noted.

### LEVEL 11: Storytelling for educational and business content
**Rule:** the viewer leaves with a feeling, something they can use, a reason to trust you, and a reason to come back.
**Checkpoints:**
- [ ] The lesson **comes out of the story**; there's no lecture break.
- [ ] There's **a reusable tool**, a named rule they can save (e.g. "THE ENEMY TEST"), as a lesson card or line.
- [ ] The **strongest objection** is raised by us and answered honestly.
- [ ] **Sources are visible**: on-screen tags for key claims, full sources in the caption, plus an honesty line where the evidence has limits.
- [ ] **Value density:** something new every ~7–10 s.
- [ ] Each action has a trigger: **save** (the tool), **share** ("send this to…", positive), **comment** (an open question), **follow** (a named series, or a strong reason to come back).
- [ ] It stays neutral or respectful where the topic divides people.
- [ ] Captions are written for each platform you post on.

### LEVEL 12: Break it down, then write your own
**Rule:** X-ray great stories to learn their shape; write every new script from the 10 questions and the beat sheet, then X-ray your own draft.
**Checkpoints:**
- [ ] The **10 questions** (the 10 questions below) were answered before drafting.
- [ ] The draft follows the **beat sheet**, or deliberately varies its shape (e.g. doubt first) so the series doesn't feel repetitive.
- [ ] The **X-ray scorecard** was run on the finished draft, every level was scored, and the weakest 2 were fixed.
- [ ] **Shape is borrowed, content never is.** When you study a reference, steal its structure only.

## The 10 questions + the beat sheet

**Answer these 10 before writing:**
1. What does the viewer believe now (the **before**)?
2. Who or what is the **surprising witness, source or angle**?
3. **Who wants what, and what do they lose** if they don't get it (Level 2)?
4. What's the **one scene** we slow down for?
5. What's the **reveal word or image**, and the **motif**?
6. What's the **strongest objection**?
7. What answers it, **weak → strong → strongest**?
8. What's the **irony** or turn?
9. What's the **tool** they can save?
10. What's the **"not X, but Y" flip** at the end?

**Beat sheet: Short (about 60 s)**

| Time | Beat | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 s | HOOK | before-belief or strange object + contradiction; face or motion in frame 1; text on screen |
| 4–6 s | BRIDGE | one line that opens the story loop |
| 6–12 s | SCENE 1 | fast; the old event; one vivid detail; the stakes rise |
| 12–30 s | SCENE 2 | slow; the witness; inner conflict; delayed reveal |
| 30–36 s | DOUBT | the strongest objection; stakes said; colour drains |
| 36–45 s | CLIMB | evidence weak → strong → strongest |
| 45–49 s | IRONY | the theme felt |
| 49–57 s | PAYOFF | "not X, but Y"; callback; signature line |
| last 3 s | TOOL CARD | named rule + next-episode tease |

**Long form (8–20 min):** the same 12 levels at two scales.
- **The whole video** has one main loop, one theme, one big drop around 60–70% of the runtime, and a final reframe.
- **Every chapter (about 2–3 min)** is its own mini-story: a chapter hook, a scene, a turn, a mini-payoff, and a loop into the next chapter.
- Re-hook every ~60–90 s, and put the tool and the series CTA at the end.
- **Sleep/calm channels:** keep loops and scenes, but use soft escalation, no shocks, slow pacing (~120–130 wpm), and an ending that settles rather than cliffhangs.

**Clip tabs (podcast or owner audio fixed):** you can't rewrite the words, so apply the levels in **selection** (choose a clip that already has problem / turn / payoff) and in the **edit** (hook text, scenes in b-roll, freeze or rewind reveals, a lesson card, captions).
