# Shots first, hooks tested

The 12 levels make the script good. They don't catch the three things that actually lose viewers in short video: **frame 1, length, and telling instead of showing.** This file closes those gaps.

## 1. Shots-first table

Write the film first and the words second. Every row is one shot of about 1 to 2 seconds. If the words column is empty for a row, good: the picture is doing the work.

| # | Sec | SEE (shot, camera, subject) | HEAR: voice | HEAR: SFX / music | TEXT on screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.0–1.2 | Extreme close-up, a silver cup fills the frame; a hand drops it, the handle SNAPS, freeze | — | crack (hit), music off | **$1,280** |
| 2 | 1.2–2.6 | Whip-pan to the host holding the broken handle, deadpan | That just cost twelve hundred eighty dollars. | low drone in | — |

**Rules**
1. **Write the SEE column for the whole video before any voice-over.** Read only the SEE column: if the story makes sense muted, it's ready for words.
2. **One idea per shot, a new shot every 1 to 2 s.** That's 15 to 25 shots in a 30 to 40 s Short. Nothing holds longer than 3 s unless it's the slow scene on purpose.
3. **Every fact is an action.** Don't write "the factories stopped". Show the factory lights clicking off one by one. If a fact can't be shown, cut it.
4. **Camera on every shot:** push-in, pull-out, whip-pan, top-down, match cut, rack focus. Never two static shots in a row.
5. **3 depth layers in wide shots** (foreground, subject, background) so the camera has something to move through.
6. **The sound column is mandatory.** Every action gets a sound. Mark the music build, the silence before the reveal, then the hit.
7. **On-screen text is 1 to 3 words, huge.** Captions go off on punchlines so the picture lands.
8. **Count.** About 90 to 110 voice-over words for a 30 to 40 s Short. Cut lines before you speed up the voice.
9. **The muted test.** A stranger watching only the SEE and TEXT columns must be able to retell the video in one sentence.

## 2. Hook testing (never trust the first idea)

1. Write **15 pairs of frame 1 + first line**, using at least 5 types: in the middle of the action, the object + an absurd number, a contradiction, a visual question, a "wrong" image (something out of place), a reaction, a point-of-view shot.
2. **Score each from 1 to 10:** understood muted in 0.5 s? One huge image? Motion at frame 0? Opens a question the viewer can't guess? Fully paid off later (no overpromise)?
3. **Blind test the top 5.** Give a fresh agent ONLY the frame description and the first line, with no script and no topic. Ask: "What is this about? Would you stop? What do you expect next?" Any hook it misreads is out.
4. Pick the winner and keep the runner-up as the A/B alternative.

**Frame-1 rules**
- One object or face, filling about 60% of the frame.
- At most one piece of text, huge.
- Something is already moving at 0.0 s (falling, cracking, printing, walking).
- The voice starts within 0.2 s.
- No logo, no title card, no intro.
- A context cue that tells the viewer the world instantly (a uniform, a place, a stencil). Without it, a coffee cup reads as a kitchen ad.

## 3. Hidden-views test (the quality bar)

Before calling a video finished: take yours plus 4 real videos in the same niche with 1M+ views. Hide titles, channels and view counts, shuffle them, and ask a fresh blind reviewer to rank them by how many views each probably has and to name the one that feels cheapest. **Pass** = yours ranks top 3 and isn't named the cheapest. **Fail** = fix what was named, then test again.

## 4. Learn from every post

At 48 hours and 7 days, line the retention graph up with your shots-first table. Mark every drop of more than 5 points against its shot number, write the cause (a dead shot, a slow line, a confusing picture), and turn it into a rule in a taste log you read before every new script. Every time the creator reacts to a moment ("love this", "boring", "too fast"), log it as a rule too.
